# Polling Pops product demo script

This script accompanies the automated product walkthrough generated by
`tests/demo/pollingpops-demo.spec.ts`. The scenario is fictional and uses only local, synthetic data.

The original recording is a historical walkthrough of the classic Recovery Loop, not a current
feature inventory or release-readiness demonstration. Its automated scenario does not exercise
Question Health, decision replay capture, whole-room live flex timing, structured Presentation
slides, or permanent Library/session deletion. Polling Pops has no published release or public
hosted-service availability claim; production and institutional gates remain open. Optional beta
workflows require their deployment flags and workspace allowlists. See the
[current user guide](user-guide.md) and [beta configuration](quick-start.md#optional-beta-workflows).

The narration below is the documentation target for a refreshed video. The renderer and caption
generator keep separate embedded copies, so editing this file alone does not update an existing
recording, narration track, or captions.

## 1. Product promise

Hi, and welcome to Polling Pops. Let me show you what makes it a little different from a typical live
quiz. The goal isn't just to collect answers or rank a room. It's to notice where understanding
breaks down, respond while everyone is still together, and then check whether that response
actually helped. We call that the Recovery Loop: ask, diagnose, intervene, recheck, and prove.

## 2. Ask: author and launch

Let's imagine I'm leading a short safety refresher. I sign in and create a checkpoint set about
completion rates. For this question, I want more than the selected answer, so I require a
confidence rating and tag the concept I'm checking. Seventy percent represents a common
part-versus-whole mistake, so I add a private misconception label and feedback that participants
will see only after reveal. Then I add a linked recheck using different numbers. That matters:
later, I can see whether people transferred the idea, instead of simply changing their vote. Once
everything is saved, I publish an immutable version, review the room defaults, and create the live
session. Polling Pops gives me a seven-digit code, a direct link, and a downloadable QR code, so
people can join from the device already in their hand.

## 3. Join and respond

Now let's switch to Taylor's view. Taylor scans the QR code, or enters the code, and joins without
creating an account. Taylor can choose a nickname or leave it blank for a generated one. The host
can also require generated nicknames for the room. Taylor has a question. It goes into the facilitator's moderation queue, and
the reply appears here without interrupting the round. When the checkpoint opens, Taylor chooses
seventy percent and says, very sure. That confidence signal is important: a confident wrong answer
usually calls for a different response than a hesitant guess. Polling Pops saves the response before
the confirmation appears.

## 4. Diagnose and intervene

Back on the facilitator screen, all five people have answered. Four chose the same wrong answer,
and they were confident about it. When I lock responses, Polling Pops shows the exact measurement
behind its suggestion: the sample size, participation, and correctness rate. This isn't an AI
verdict. It's a transparent rule, and I still decide what makes sense for this group. I'll start
with a brief peer discussion, then reveal the answer and explanation. I can also record that I gave
a targeted explanation. Those interventions become part of the session timeline, so the report
captures not only the outcome, but what I actually did before rechecking.

## 5. Recheck

Now I open the linked recheck. The numbers have changed, but the concept hasn't. Taylor applies the
completed-over-total relationship and chooses eighty percent. Improvement on a fresh example is
stronger evidence than changing an answer on the original question. Polling Pops supports both, but
reports linked rechecks and revotes separately, so we don't overstate what happened.

## 6. Prove and follow up

Once the round finishes, the evidence is ready. Initial accuracy was twenty percent. Of the four
people who started incorrect and answered both checkpoints, three recovered on the linked recheck.
Polling Pops shows that numerator and denominator, along with the small-sample warning, because this
is useful session evidence, not proof of long-term learning. I can also inspect confidence versus
correctness, the observed misconception, the intervention timeline, and the one concept that
remains unresolved. From there, I can create a time-flex follow-up. Each participant gets a private,
revocable link; there's also an anonymous option. If someone needs extra time, I can create a
one-point-five or two-times accommodation pass without storing a medical reason or exposing it to
the group.

## 7. Use cases and close

So, where does this fit? University lectures are an obvious example, but the same pattern works for
technical training, safety briefings, compliance refreshers, and certification review. Anywhere a
facilitator needs to know what landed, and has time to do something about it, Polling Pops can help.
Participants join without accounts, and the repository includes an Apache-licensed Community stack
for local self-hosting. Polling Pops is still pre-release; the professional workspace and other beta
capabilities require an operator-enabled rollout, and public production readiness is still being
established. That's Polling Pops: ask, diagnose, intervene, recheck, and prove. Thanks for watching.

## Optional beta demonstration inserts

These are planning notes for a future synthetic capture, not chapters present in the original
recording:

- **Authoring review:** review a saved draft with Question Health, inspect the rule and evidence,
  dismiss/reopen one matching finding, and preview/apply a field revision with immediate undo.
  Review the immutable published version separately. Use synthetic retained reports meeting the
  sample limits for post-use observations; a five-person demo cannot demonstrate those flags.
- **Flexible live timing:** choose whole-room **Flex** before creating a Round or eligible live
  Presentation. Show that responses remain open without a deadline or speed bonus until the host
  closes them. Do not confuse this with the self-paced time-flex follow-up shown in chapter 6.
- **Recorded decisions:** create a Round while decision replay capture is enabled, then show the
  report's recorded insight measurement and facilitator-action timeline. Label missing or partial
  history and avoid claiming that an intervention caused recovery.
- **Structured slides:** create a Presentation with content and question blocks. Show the six

  Show that archive is reversible and confirmed deletion is not.

## Regenerating the product-demo screen recording

Before publishing a refreshed video, update the embedded narration in
`scripts/demo/render-pollingpops-demo.sh` and the narration/caption copy in
`scripts/demo/write-pollingpops-captions.py` to match this script, including the pre-release close.
If adding beta chapters, also update the automated capture and renderer chapter list with the
required local rollout configuration. Then regenerate the recording, audio, and captions together
and inspect them for current labels, availability claims, and correspondence with the screen.
This documentation update does not regenerate those media artifacts.

Install the isolated media tools once:

```bash
npm install --prefix /tmp/pollingpops-video-tools ffmpeg-static
python3 -m venv /tmp/pollingpops-voice-tools
/tmp/pollingpops-voice-tools/bin/pip install edge-tts
```

Then record and render:

```bash
pnpm exec playwright test --config=playwright.demo.config.ts
./scripts/demo/render-pollingpops-demo.sh
```

The raw chapter recordings are written to `artifacts/pollingpops-demo/raw`. Narration, captions, and
the final MP4 are assembled by `scripts/demo/render-pollingpops-demo.sh`. The renderer uses the
conversational `en-US-AvaMultilingualNeural` voice by default; set `OPENROUND_VOICE` to any voice
listed by `edge-tts --list-voices` to choose another presenter. Neural narration requires a network
connection and sends only its embedded synthetic narration to the speech service; it does not
send participant or product data.

## Regenerating the onboarding video guides

The Help centre uses two shorter, captioned guides: **Quick start: create your first Round** and
**Workspace and builder guide**. Their original product captures live in
`scripts/demo/assets/guides`. The written guidance in
`apps/web/app/help/help-guidance.tsx` describes current supported behavior, including the bounded
slide editor; this documentation update has not regenerated the existing MP4s, posters, or WebVTT
captions. Before publishing refreshed professional guides, align that written copy with the
embedded narration in `scripts/demo/render-onboarding-guides.sh`, replace captures where the
editor or workflow has changed, and regenerate narration, captions, and media together. Inspect
the result for current labels, rollout availability, and agreement between written guidance,
spoken narration, captions, and the screen.

Install FFmpeg with `ffprobe`, plus the isolated narration tool shown above, then run:

```bash
OPENROUND_FFMPEG="$(command -v ffmpeg)" \
OPENROUND_FFPROBE="$(command -v ffprobe)" \
OPENROUND_EDGE_TTS=/tmp/pollingpops-voice-tools/bin/edge-tts \
./scripts/demo/render-onboarding-guides.sh
```

The renderer writes temporary chapter media beneath `artifacts/onboarding-guides` and publishes the
MP4, poster image, and English WebVTT captions to `apps/web/public/guides`. Set `OPENROUND_VOICE` to
another voice reported by `edge-tts --list-voices` when needed.

Only use local captures containing synthetic, non-secret content. Inspect every replacement frame
for email addresses, names, tokens, room codes, and private workspace material before committing it.
Rendering sends the project-authored narration text to the configured text-to-speech service; it
does not send the screenshots, application state, participant responses, or other product data.
